### PRE-HISTORY & FULL HISTORIC DATA SCRAPE (Venice Context)
Venice’s lagoon roots trace to 5th–8th century AD Byzantine-style settlements with scattered private “squeri” (small wooden boat yards along canals for basic building/repairs). These were not centralized or industrial — just local maintenance for fishing/trade craft. No evidence of organized “haul-out” infrastructure pre-1100.

**The Venetian Arsenal (Arsenale di Venezia)**: The global benchmark.
- **Founded**: Construction officially began ~1104 AD (during Doge Ordelafo Faliero’s reign); possible Byzantine precursor as early as 8th century, but confirmed existence by early 13th century.
- **Evolution**: Started as state dockyard for maintaining private ships (early 12th c.). Expanded massively — Arsenale Nuovo (1320), further enlargements to 1470–1570 (total ~45 hectares / 110 acres, ~15% of Venice). Peak 16th century: proto-industrial assembly line via canals (ships moved between stations for framing, planking, rigging, arming). Produced up to 1 ship/day + full provisioning; employed 16,000 workers. Built/maintained thousands of galleys, galleasses, merchant vessels. Included rope factory, munitions, standardized parts.
- **Haul-Out / Marina Role**: Canals + basins functioned as medieval “travel lifts” — ships hauled/ floated in for repairs, outfitting, winter storage. Largest integrated shipyard + armory complex in Europe pre-Industrial Revolution.
- **Peak & Global Significance**: Largest industrial site in the world by 1500; enabled Venice’s Mediterranean dominance. Dante described it in *Inferno* as a model of efficiency.
- **Decline**: Fell with Republic in 1797 (Napoleon); parts destroyed, later partially rebuilt.
- **Current Status (2026)**: NOT a working yacht marina or haul-out facility. Repurposed for MOSE flood barriers control center, Italian Navy base, Venice Biennale exhibitions, research, and historic boat preservation. No travel lifts, slips for private yachts, or refit services. Some 2019–2020s efforts to revive “boating industry” in historic tese (sheds), but nothing operational for yachts.

Closest ancient precedents are small Egyptian/Greek yards (~3000–500 BC) or Roman ports — none scaled or state-run like Arsenal. Later continuous shipyards (e.g., Kraljevica, Croatia 1729) are modern by comparison. Venice’s Arsenal is the undisputed “oldest in the world” for this category.
