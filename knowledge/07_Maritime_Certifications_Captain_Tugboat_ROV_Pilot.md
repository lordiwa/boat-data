**Maritime and offshore certifications** involve sea time/experience, approved training courses, written/practical exams, medical checks, drug tests, and safety certifications like **STCW** or **BOSIET/HUET**. Requirements vary by country (e.g., USCG in the US), flag state, and specific role. Here's a breakdown focused on captain roles, tugboats, sub/ROV drivers (common on oil platforms), helicopter pilots, and related areas.

### 1. Ship Captain / Master Licenses (General)
Common entry points include **USCG (U.S. Coast Guard)** credentials for domestic or near-coastal work.

- **OUPV (Operator of Uninspected Passenger Vessels, "6-Pack")**: For small passenger vessels (<100 GT, up to 6 paying passengers).
  - **Sea time**: 360 days (90 in last 3–7 years; some near-coastal/ocean required for broader endorsement).
  - **Age**: 18+.
  - **Exams/Training**: USCG-approved course (several days) covering navigation rules, chart plotting, deck general/safety, seamanship. Proctored exam (often passed via approved school).
  - Other: TWIC card, physical/medical exam, drug test, CPR/First Aid, character references.

- **Master 25/50/100 Gross Tons**: Broader commercial command.
  - More sea time (e.g., 360–720 days depending on waters/tonnage).
  - Similar course + exams, often with upgrades.

**STCW (Standards of Training, Certification, and Watchkeeping)** is often required for international or larger vessels: Basic Safety Training (5 modules/days: personal survival, fire prevention, first aid, etc.). Advanced courses for officers.

### 2. Tugboat Captain / Master of Towing
Tugboat roles often build on a captain's license with a **Towing Endorsement**.

- Start as deckhand/mate → accumulate experience.
- **Sea time**: Often 540 days as mate of towing vessels (90 on one route).
- **Training/Exams**: Captain's course + **Towing Officer Assessment Record (TOAR)** practical assessment for piloting tugs (ocean/inland).
- Additional: Specific towing maneuvers, stability, etc. Many progress via apprenticeships.

### 3. Sub Driver / ROV Pilot (Remotely Operated Vehicle) on Oil Platforms
"Sub driver" typically refers to **ROV Pilot/Technician** roles in offshore oil/gas (underwater vehicles for inspection, maintenance, intervention).

- **Entry**: Technical background (electronics, mechanics, hydraulics, engineering degree/tech training preferred). No massive sea time initially, but offshore experience helps.
- **Training/Certification**:
  - Manufacturer or specialized ROV schools (e.g., 4-week Commercial Oilfield ROV Pilot Technician courses covering IMCA competencies).
  - IMCA (International Marine Contractors Association) framework: Grades like ROV Pilot Tech Grade II (entry), then Senior Pilot, Supervisor. Involves logbooks, assessments.
  - Hands-on: Systems operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, tether management, piloting.
- **Offshore Safety** (mandatory): BOSIET (Basic Offshore Safety Induction and Emergency Training), HUET (Helicopter Underwater Escape), medical certificate, often MIST.
- Progression: Start as trainee/tech, build hours in control suite.

### 4. Helicopter Pilot on Oil Platforms (Offshore)
- **Licensing**: Commercial Helicopter Pilot License (e.g., via FAA or equivalent) + significant flight hours (often 1,000–1,500+ for offshore roles).
- **Specific Training**: Offshore survival (HUET/BOSIET), instrument flying, night operations, platform landings.
- Exams include written knowledge tests, practical flight checks.

### Other Common Certified Areas in Maritime/Offshore
- **Deck/Engine Officers**: Higher STCW endorsements, longer sea time (e.g., 12–36 months), exams for Watchkeeping Officer → Chief Mate → Master.
- **Dynamic Positioning (DP) Operators**: For vessels/platforms — specialized courses + simulator exams.
- **Diving / Saturation Diving**: Commercial dive certs + offshore safety.
- **Crane Operators / Riggers**: Offshore-specific crane tickets.
- **Safety / HSE Roles**: BOSIET, HUET, firefighting, first aid refreshers (valid 3–4 years).
- **Engineers / Technicians**: Specific machinery endorsements, electrical/hydraulic certs.

### General Requirements Across Roles
- **Medical/Physical**: USCG or OGUK medical, color vision, drug screening.
- **TWIC Card** (US): Transportation Worker ID.
- **Renewals**: Sea time, refresher courses, exams.
- **International**: STCW for most flag states; flag-specific endorsements.

**Advice**: Requirements differ by location (US, North Sea, Gulf of Mexico, etc.) and employer. Start with entry-level sea time or approved courses from USCG-approved schools. Check official sites like USCG National Maritime Center for checklists. For ROV/offshore, IMCA and OPITO standards dominate.

## Grok

**USCG licenses (and similar in other countries) specify "routes" or "waters" endorsements** that define exactly where you can sail/command a vessel. These are printed on your Merchant Mariner Credential (MMC) or license.

### Main Sailing / Operating Areas (Routes) on a License
Here are the primary categories, from most restricted to broadest:

- **Inland Waters** — Bays, sounds, rivers, harbors, and protected waters **inside** the USCG boundary lines (COLREGS Demarcation Lines). Limited to sheltered/coastal areas.

- **Great Lakes** — Specific endorsement for the Great Lakes and connecting waters. Often combined with Inland.

- **Near Coastal** (most common for captains) — Typically up to **100 nautical miles offshore** (sometimes noted as 200 miles depending on the exact endorsement). Allows operation beyond inland waters but not full ocean voyages. Many OUPV ("6-pack") and Master 25/50/100 ton licenses are issued as **Near Coastal**.

- **Oceans** — Unlimited offshore, any ocean waters. Highest level; qualifies you for **any waters** (except some Western Rivers towing restrictions).

**Hierarchy note**: Oceans > Near Coastal > Great Lakes/Inland. An Oceans endorsement generally lets you operate on all waters (with exceptions). A Near Coastal one covers Great Lakes + Inland.

### Other Common Endorsements on the License
- **Towing Endorsement** (for tugboats) — Can have its own route (Inland, Near Coastal/Oceans, Western Rivers). Requires extra sea time + TOAR (practical assessment). Western Rivers often needs 90 days specific training.

- **Sailing / Auxiliary Sail Endorsement** — Added to your Master license for sailing vessels or auxiliary sail (sail + motor). Inherits the same waters/routes as your base license. Requires extra sea time on sail vessels (e.g., 180 days for Inland Master, 360 days for Near Coastal) + a short course/exam.

- **Specific Limitations** — Your license may list restrictions like "Not valid for Western Rivers," tonnage limits, or geographic areas based on your sea time.

### How You Get the Area on Your License
- It depends on **where your sea time was accumulated**:
  - Time inside boundary lines → Inland credit.
  - Time outside (offshore) → Near Coastal / Oceans credit.
  - Great Lakes time counts favorably toward higher routes.
- When applying, you submit sea service letters proving the waters. The National Maritime Center (NMC) evaluates and issues the appropriate route.

For tugboats or offshore platform support vessels, the route + towing endorsement matters a lot. International work often adds **STCW** endorsements.

## Grok

**International maritime licenses** are primarily governed by the **STCW Convention** (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers), administered by the **International Maritime Organization (IMO)**. STCW sets minimum global standards for training, certification, and watchkeeping on seagoing vessels (especially those ≥500 GT engaged in international voyages).

Unlike the USCG system (national endorsements + STCW add-ons), most countries issue a **Certificate of Competency (CoC)** directly aligned with STCW regulations. The CoC is issued by the flag state or your home country's maritime administration and is recognized internationally (with possible flag-state endorsements required for specific ships).

### Key Distinctions: STCW vs. National/Flag-State Documents
- **STCW Certificates/Endorsements**: Prove you have the required training, competence, and assessments (operational or management level). These are internationally standardized.
- **Flag-State Endorsement / Recognition**: The ship’s flag (e.g., Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands) often requires an additional endorsement or "attestation" on top of your CoC/STCW. This is usually quick (paper or electronic) once you have a valid CoC.
- **National License/CoC**: Your primary qualification from your country’s authority.

### Main STCW Deck Officer Certifications (Relevant to Captain/Master Roles)
Progression follows STCW Chapters II (Deck) and III (Engine):

- **Rating Forming Part of Navigational Watch (RFPNW) / Able Seafarer Deck** — Entry-level ratings (STCW II/4 & II/5).
- **Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch (OICNW / Watchkeeping Officer)** — Operational level (STCW II/1). Equivalent to USCG Mate/OUPV upgrades. Requires approved training, sea time (e.g., 12 months as cadet or watchkeeping), and assessments.
- **Chief Mate** — Management level (STCW II/2). For vessels 500–3,000 GT or ≥3,000 GT.
- **Master (Captain)** — Highest management level (STCW II/2). Sea time often 36 months as OICNW (or reduced with prior Chief Mate time) + advanced training.

**Tonnage & Voyage Limitations** exist (e.g., <500 GT near-coastal, 500–3,000 GT, unlimited ≥3,000 GT, oceans vs. near-coastal).

### Other Specialized International Certifications
- **Towing / Tugboat**: STCW towing competencies often added via specific training and assessments (similar to USCG TOAR). Many flags require Master/Mate with towing endorsement.
- **Offshore Supply Vessel (OSV) / Platform Support**: STCW II/1 or II/2 with OSV-specific modules.
- **MODU (Mobile Offshore Drilling Units)**: Offshore Installation Manager (OIM), Barge Supervisor, Ballast Control Operator — often under IMO resolutions with flag-state approvals.
- **ROV Pilot/Technician** (sub driver on oil platforms): Not strictly STCW but follows **IMCA (International Marine Contractors Association)** standards. Introductory courses + Grade II/III progression with logbooks, competencies in piloting, maintenance, etc. Technical background preferred.
- **Dynamic Positioning (DP) Operator**: Specialized courses (Induction + Basic/Advanced) + sea time for offshore vessels/platforms.
- **Sailing / Yacht**: MCA Master 200GT or similar (common for superyachts), with STCW.

### Common Mandatory STCW Courses (Basic & Advanced)
- **Basic Safety Training (BST)**: 5–6 days (Personal Survival, Fire Prevention/Fighting, Elementary First Aid, Personal Safety & Social Responsibilities, sometimes Security). Required for almost everyone.
- **Advanced Firefighting**, **Proficiency in Survival Craft & Rescue Boats (PSCRB)**, **Medical Care**, etc.
- **GMDSS** (radio), **ARPA/Radar**, **ECDIS**, **Bridge Resource Management**, etc., for officers.
- **Helicopter Underwater Escape Training (HUET)** + **BOSIET** for offshore work.

Refresher training is required every 5 years for many.

### How to Obtain International Licenses
1. Complete approved maritime academy/training courses.
2. Accumulate documented sea time (logbooks).
3. Pass assessments/exams (written, practical, simulator, oral).
4. Apply to your national administration for CoC.
5. Get flag-state endorsement for the specific ship.

**Exams & Assessments**: Vary by country but cover navigation, stability, meteorology, COLREGS, cargo, management, etc. Many include simulator checks and onboard assessments.

### Popular Systems & Equivalencies
- **UK MCA (Master & Chief Mate, Yacht, etc.)**: Widely recognized; good for superyachts and international work.
- **USCG**: National + STCW endorsements; accepted with flag endorsements.
- **Other**: Australia, Norway, Panama/Liberia (open registers — easier endorsements), etc.
- Equivalencies exist (e.g., USCG 500/1600 GT often maps to MCA Class IV or similar).

**For Ecuador (your location)**: Check with Dirección General de la Marina Mercante (or equivalent) for national CoC. Many work on international flags via STCW.

**Renewals**: Typically 5 years; requires refresher courses and sometimes re-assessment.

STCW is the global baseline — your USCG credentials can often be converted or endorsed for international service. Requirements are rigorous with sea time, approved courses, and competence demonstrations.
