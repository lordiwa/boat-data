### **Overview of the Yacht Building Industry**

The yacht building industry is a niche sector within the broader maritime industry, focused on constructing luxury vessels for private use, chartering, or occasionally racing. Yachts are generally categorized by size, with the term "superyacht" typically applying to vessels over 24 meters (79 feet) in length, and "megayachts" or "gigayachts" referring to those exceeding 60 meters (197 feet) or even 100 meters (328 feet). The industry is global, with major hubs in Europe (Italy, Netherlands, Germany), the United States, Turkey, the Middle East, and Australasia.

The global yacht market was valued at USD 11.19 billion in 2022 and is projected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6.6% through 2030, driven by demand for sustainable yachts, advanced technology, and increased chartering. The industry is highly specialized, with shipyards competing on craftsmanship, innovation, customization, and prestige.[](https://www.emergenresearch.com/blog/top-10-companies-in-yacht-market-in-2024)

Yachts are built in three main categories:
1. **Fully Custom**: Designed and built from scratch to the owner’s specifications, often taking 3–5 years or more. These are the most expensive and prestigious.
2. **Semi-Custom**: Based on a pre-existing platform or hull design but with customizable interiors, layouts, and features. These offer a balance of personalization and faster delivery (1–3 years).
3. **Production Series**: Standardized models with limited customization, built in series for quicker delivery (6 months–2 years). These are typically smaller yachts (under 40 meters).

Yachts are further divided by type:
- **Motor Yachts**: Powered by engines, ranging from sleek, high-speed vessels to massive, long-range cruisers.
- **Sailing Yachts**: Powered primarily by sails, often combining luxury with performance.
- **Explorer Yachts**: Designed for long-range cruising, often in remote areas like the Arctic or Antarctica.
- **Catamarans**: Multi-hulled vessels, popular for stability and space, especially in the charter market.
- **Sportfishing Yachts**: Specialized for fishing, common in the U.S. and Caribbean markets.

### **What Do They Build?**

Custom yacht manufacturers build vessels tailored to the owner’s specifications, ranging from aesthetic preferences to technical requirements. The types of yachts and materials used vary by builder and region:

1. **Motor Yachts**:
   - **Description**: The most popular category, ranging from 24m to over 100m. Includes displacement yachts (long-range, fuel-efficient), semi-displacement yachts (balanced speed and range), and planing yachts (high-speed, sporty).
   - **Materials**: Steel (for hulls of larger yachts), aluminum (lightweight, used in high-performance yachts), fiberglass/GRP (common in semi-custom and production yachts), and carbon fiber (for lightweight, high-performance vessels).
   - **Examples**: Lürssen’s Azzam (592ft, motor megayacht), Benetti’s Lana (100m+, custom motor yacht), Heesen’s Galactica Super Nova (80m, aluminum semi-custom).

2. **Sailing Yachts**:
   - **Description**: Range from performance-oriented vessels to luxury cruisers, typically 24m–90m. Custom sailing yachts are less common but highly prestigious.
   - **Materials**: Aluminum, carbon fiber, and composite materials for lightweight, high-performance hulls; steel for larger vessels.
   - **Examples**: Royal Huisman’s Sea Eagle II (81m, custom sailing yacht), Perini Navi’s Maltese Falcon (88m, iconic sailing yacht).

3. **Explorer Yachts**:
   - **Description**: Designed for long-range cruising in challenging environments (e.g., polar regions). Often feature reinforced hulls and extensive fuel capacity.
   - **Materials**: Steel for durability, aluminum for weight savings.
   - **Examples**: Damen’s SeaXplorer series, Cantiere delle Marche’s RJ series.

4. **Catamarans**:
   - **Description**: Multi-hulled vessels offering stability and space, popular in the charter market. Custom catamarans are a growing segment.
   - **Materials**: Fiberglass, carbon fiber, aluminum.
   - **Examples**: Sunreef’s 80 Power Catamaran, Lagoon’s custom catamarans.

5. **Sportfishing Yachts**:
   - **Description**: Specialized for fishing, common in the U.S. and Caribbean. Custom builds focus on performance and luxury.
   - **Materials**: Fiberglass, composite materials.
   - **Examples**: Viking’s 92 Convertible, Rybovich’s custom sportfishers.

6. **Support Vessels**:
   - **Description**: Shadow vessels that carry toys (e.g., helicopters, submersibles) and crew for megayachts. A growing niche in custom builds.
   - **Materials**: Steel, aluminum.
   - **Examples**: Damen’s 250ft support vessel for Oceanco’s Koru.

**Customization Features**:
- **Interiors**: Owners specify everything from materials (e.g., rare woods, marble, gold leaf) to layouts (e.g., private cinemas, spas, beach clubs).
- **Technology**: Advanced navigation, hybrid propulsion, zero-emission systems, and smart home integration.
- **Amenities**: Helicopter pads, infinity pools, submersibles, and even onboard hospitals.
- **Sustainability**: Increasing demand for eco-friendly yachts with hybrid engines, solar panels, and lightweight materials to reduce fuel consumption.
