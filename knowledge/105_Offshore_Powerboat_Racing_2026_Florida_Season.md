## Florida-Hosted Championship Structure

- **APBA National Offshore Championship:** the 2026 six-round series includes three Florida rounds (Cocoa Beach, Clearwater, Englewood) and finishes at Key West.
- **RWO 2026 season:** RWO runs Atlantic City, Michigan City, Clearwater, and Key West; Class 1 and Class 2 score toward both APBA national and UIM world titles.
- **UIM World Championship classes:** Class 1, Class 2, and Super Cat race through the RWO schedule, with the title decided at Key West.
- **Super Cat owners' series:** Dirty Money Racing led the six-race owners' standings ahead of CR Racing, Graydel, EWB, and Howe2Live Offshore.

## History Section Outline (flagship, story-driven)

Structure: origins → Prohibition and rum-running → modern sport → Italy and Europe → Don Aronow and Cigarette → Class 1 and today. Core dates: powerboat competition began with the 1903 Harmsworth Trophy; the first true offshore race ran England to Calais in 1904; the modern offshore era began with Miami–Nassau in 1956; UIM sanctioned Class 1 in 1964.

1. **Before offshore racing: engines reach the water.** The British International Trophy (Harmsworth Trophy) of 1903 at the Royal Cork Yacht Club, Queenstown, Ireland, conceived as a contest between nations. In 1904 the Automobile Club of Great Britain and Ireland and its Marine Motor Association ran a race from England's southeast coast to Calais, widely treated as the first recognized offshore powerboat race.
2. **Rum running: the boats that shaped the myth.** During Prohibition (1920–1933) fast "Bimini boats" ran liquor from the Bahamas to Florida, some reaching the coast in about two hours. The Cigarette name came from a Prohibition-era rum-runner's launch. Editorial framing: rum-running influenced the culture, names, and performance-boat market; organized offshore racing was not founded by smugglers.
3. **The modern sport begins: Miami–Nassau, 1956.** Sam Griffith originated the roughly 184–185 mile challenge across the Gulf Stream; first race May 6, 1956. In 1960 Dick Bertram's 30-foot Moppie, with a Ray Hunt deep-V hull, driven by Griffith and Bertram, won the 185-mile race, proving the deep-V concept. Sherman "Red" Crise staged an earlier Miami-to-Nassau ocean race in 1954; present both carefully.
4. **Post-war boom: Florida becomes the center.** Don Aronow founded Formula Marine in Miami in 1962, beginning the dynasty of Donzi, Magnum, and Cigarette. Page elements: Thunderboat Row on NE 188th Street, fiberglass replacing wood, Jim Wynne's sterndrive.
5. **Europe joins: Cowes–Torquay, 1961.** Sir Max Aitken announced the race at the January 1961 London Boat Show, sponsored by the Daily Express. First run August 19, 1961: 27 boats started, nine finished in time; Tommy Sopwith won aboard a Campbell 25-footer.
6. **Italy and the Mediterranean.** UIM sanctioned Class 1 in 1964; the first Class 1 world title went to Jim Wynne aboard Wynne-Daytona. In the 1980s Don Shead's aluminum monohulls, Italian builders Picchiotti and CUV, and the James Beard–Clive Curtis Cougar catamarans set new standards.
7. **Don Aronow.** Born March 3, 1927; died February 3, 1987, Miami. Entered the 1962 Miami–Nassau race, hired Jim Wynne and Walt Walters, produced the Formula 233 deep-V. Founded Formula, Donzi, Magnum Marine (sold 1968), Cigarette Racing Team (1970), Squadron XII, USA Racing Team. World Powerboat Champion 1967, three US championships. Boats associated with him are credited with 10 world championships, 25 US championships, about 300 international race victories, and 25 world records.
8. **Cigarette timeline.** 1962 Formula 233 development; 1963 Miami plant on NE 188th Street; 1969 the narrow-hulled Cigarette emerges; 1970 Cigarette Racing Team founded; 1980s Miami Vice-era fame; 2021 sold to Lionheart Capital and John H. Ruiz.
9. **Class 1 and the global championship era.** Technical evolution from wooden displacement hulls through Ray Hunt deep-Vs, fiberglass production racers, 1980s aluminum monohulls and Italian design, Cougar-style catamarans, to modern canopied Class 1 catamarans near 160 mph.
10. **Florida today: from history to Key West.** Close by linking to the 2026 calendar above; Key West is the traditional season finale continuing the Miami–Nassau lineage.
