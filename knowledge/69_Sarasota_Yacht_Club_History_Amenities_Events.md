Donald Joel Aronow (March 3, 1927 – February 3, 1987) was a towering figure in the world of powerboating, renowned for designing, building, and racing high-performance speedboats that redefined offshore racing and became cultural icons. His brands—Formula, Donzi, Magnum Marine, Cary, Cigarette, and USA Racing Team—dominated the sport, winning over 350 offshore races, two world championships, and three U.S. championships. Aronow’s boats were not only feats of engineering but also symbols of speed, luxury, and a glamorous, often dangerous lifestyle. His clients included presidents, royalty, celebrities, and, controversially, drug smugglers, reflecting the duality of his world. Tragically, his life ended in a hail of bullets on Miami’s Thunderboat Row, a street he made famous, leaving behind a legacy as complex as the man himself.

#### Early Life: From Brooklyn to Millionaire
Born in Sheepshead Bay, Brooklyn, Aronow was the youngest child of Russian-Jewish immigrants Herman and Ruth Aronow. His father ran a gas station and later a taxi company, but the Great Depression hit the family hard, shaping Aronow’s drive for success. A top athlete at James Madison High School, he excelled in football, wrestling, and track, graduating in 1944. He worked as a lifeguard at Coney Island before enrolling at Brooklyn College, only to join the United States Merchant Marine in 1945, serving overseas until World War II’s end. Returning in 1947, he completed his degree in physical education in 1948.[](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Aronow)

Aronow’s early career was unremarkable. He briefly taught physical education before joining his father-in-law’s construction business in southern New Jersey, which thrived amid post-WWII demand. In 1953, at age 26, he founded the Aronow Corporation, building homes, shopping centers, and industrial parks. By 1959, at 32, he was a millionaire, but the grind left him with ulcers and burnout. Seeking a change, he moved his family—wife Shirley Goldin and their three children (Michael, David, and Claudia)—to Miami Beach in 1960, drawn to its sunshine and slower pace.[](https://www.offshoreonly.com/forums/general-boating-discussion/74040-aronow-nutshell.html)

This move marked a turning point. Aronow bought a 40-foot sportfishing boat and began spearfishing, befriending local celebrities like Frank Satenstein, director of *The Jackie Gleason Show*. His competitive nature surfaced when he realized his friends were better boaters. Determined to outdo them, he commissioned a 21-foot wooden runabout designed by Harry Schoell, igniting his passion for boating.[](https://www.powerboatnation.com/brownies-bites-don-aronow-the-early-years-before-he-became-a-god/)

#### Entry into Powerboating: The Miami-Nassau Race
In 1962, Aronow’s friend Forrest Johnson introduced him to the Miami-Nassau-Miami powerboat race, a grueling 184-mile offshore event. Aronow competed in April 1962, leading until a clutch failure dropped him to fourth. The experience hooked him on racing, and he was dissatisfied with the wooden boat’s performance. Deciding to design his own, his first attempt—a wooden prototype—broke apart. Undeterred, he turned to fiberglass, a then-emerging material, and collaborated with naval engineers Jim Wynne and Walt Walters to create a revolutionary 23-foot deep-V hull boat. This became the foundation of his first company, Formula Marine.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/02/08/the-violent-death-of-an-american-dream/0419ae48-585a-460b-b62f-98cb0d50fddb/)[](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/industries-culture-society/who-shot-don-aronow-44036)

#### Formula Marine (1962–1964): The First Success
Founded by late 1962, Formula Marine was Aronow’s entry into boatbuilding. The Formula 233, designed by Wynne and Walters, featured a 23-foot deep-V hull with unprecedented speed (over 50 mph) and stability in rough seas. Priced at $7,985—double the cost of competitors—Aronow marketed it as a premium product, cutting dealer discounts to maintain exclusivity. He assembled a 10-boat racing team, funded by buyers, and in the 1964 Miami-Nassau race, Formulas finished fourth through tenth, with Aronow’s driver Jim Wynne winning Class III and Aronow himself taking Class II. The success drew Merrick Lewis of Alliance Machine Corp (Dayton, Ohio), who bought Formula in 1964, netting Aronow a significant profit.[](https://magnummarine.com/early-years-magnum-marines-don-aronow/)[](https://www.powerboatnation.com/brownies-bites-don-aronow-the-early-years-before-he-became-a-god/)

#### Donzi Marine (1964–1965): Exotic and Fast
With Formula sold, Aronow wasted no time. In 1964, he purchased land on NE 188th Street in North Miami Beach—later dubbed Thunderboat Row—and founded Donzi Marine. Named after himself, Donzi embodied Aronow’s flair for exotic branding. The first boat, the Donzi Sweet Sixteen, was a 16-foot speedster that became an instant hit for its speed (40–50 mph), maneuverability, and rough-water handling. Designed again by Wynne and Walters, Donzi boats featured vibrant colors and sleek lines, appealing to Miami’s flashy culture.[](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/industries-culture-society/who-shot-don-aronow-44036)

Donzi’s success was meteoric. The brand gained international acclaim, and Aronow’s boats attracted high-profile clients, including President Lyndon Johnson, who owned several 16-foot Donzis for racing Secret Service agents on his Texas ranch. In 1965, Teleflex Inc. acquired Donzi, allowing Aronow to cash out while retaining influence. His ability to build and sell companies quickly showcased his entrepreneurial genius, though some criticized his short-term focus.[](https://flasportshof.org/fshofmember/don-aronow/)[](http://youreduchoices.blogspot.com/2018/06/donald-aronow.html)

#### Magnum Marine (1966–1968): World Champion
In 1966, Aronow founded Magnum Marine, building a factory next to Donzi’s on Thunderboat Row. His goal was to dominate offshore racing. He designed the Maltese Magnum, a 27-foot race boat with a deep-V hull, which he raced to a world championship in 1967, driving a single-engine inboard and a triple-engine Mercury-powered outboard. The victory cemented his reputation as a builder and racer. Magnum expanded with models like the 35-foot Magnum, the 16-foot Marauder ski boat, and the 16-foot Missile tunnel hull, all winning races globally.[](https://magnummarine.com/project/the-beginnings/)[](https://magnummarine.com/don-aronow-world-champion-1967-maltese-magnum/)

In 1968, Aronow sold Magnum to Apeco (American Photocopy), but a strict non-compete clause barred him from building boats for several years. The sale was lucrative, but the restriction frustrated him. Magnum, under new ownership, continued to thrive, later owned by Marchesa Katrin Theodoli, who maintained its prestige.[](https://www.boatinternational.com/yachts/editorial-features/don-aronow-power-florida-boat-racing-champion-death)[](https://www.marinebusinessnews.com.au/2022/03/our-history-the-risk-takers-part-3-don-aronow/)

#### Cary and Cigarette (1969–1982): The Iconic Brand
To circumvent the non-compete clause, Aronow built boats under the name Cary in 1969, using Elton Cary’s Miami Beach facility. The first boat, a 28-foot model named *The Cigarette* after a Prohibition-era rum-running vessel, became legendary. With mechanic Norris “Knocky” House, Aronow campaigned *The Cigarette* worldwide, winning his second world championship in 1969 and a third consecutive U.S. championship. He also earned the Union Internationale Motonautique’s Gold Medal of Honor, making him the second American (after Gar Wood) to receive it.[](https://www.nautipedia.it/index.php/DON_ARONOW)[](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Aronow)

When the non-compete expired in 1970, Aronow founded the Cigarette Racing Team, formalizing the brand. Cigarette boats, ranging from 28 to 36 feet, were known for their sleek design, deep-V hulls, and blistering speeds (up to 80 mph in rough seas). The 36-foot Cigarette, considered his masterpiece, was a favorite among racers and smugglers alike due to its ability to outrun authorities. Cigarette’s success was fueled by Aronow’s charisma and sales tactics—often claiming each boat was “built for himself” to close deals.[](https://thecaptain.tv/articles/the-wild-life-of-legendary-cigarette-builder-don-aronow/)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/02/08/the-violent-death-of-an-american-dream/0419ae48-585a-460b-b62f-98cb0d50fddb/)

Cigarette boats won numerous races, including eight offshore races in 1969 alone, and became cultural icons, featured in *Miami Vice*. However, their speed made them popular with drug traffickers, leading to scrutiny. Aronow was rumored to have ties to smugglers and figures like Meyer Lansky’s syndicate, though no definitive evidence exists. He sold Cigarette in the late 1970s, repurchased it, and sold it again in 1982, reflecting his restless business style.[](https://www.mby.com/news/hit-mans-death-closes-notorious-aronow-case-9799)[](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/industries-culture-society/who-shot-don-aronow-44036)

#### USA Racing Team and Blue Thunder (1982–1987): The Final Chapter
After selling Cigarette, Aronow formed the USA Racing Team, designing the Blue Thunder, a 39-foot catamaran for the U.S. Customs Service to patrol against smugglers. Ironically, these boats chased the same Cigarettes used by drug runners. His relationship with then-Vice President George H.W. Bush reportedly helped secure the contract. The Blue Thunder was a departure from his sleek racers, prioritizing durability and functionality, but it showcased his versatility.[](https://www.offshoreonly.com/forums/general-boating-discussion/74040-aronow-nutshell.html)

#### Personal Life: Charisma and Controversy
Aronow’s personal life was as colorful as his boats. Married to Shirley Goldin in 1948, he had three children: Michael (a star athlete injured in a 1970 car accident), David, and Claudia (a New York artist). The marriage ended in divorce, and in the 1970s, he married Lillian Crawford, a former Wilhelmina model 24 years his junior, with whom he had sons Gavin and Wylie.[](https://guyanalaw.net/sealy-hybrid/don-aronow-children)[](http://youreduchoices.blogspot.com/2018/06/donald-aronow.html)

Known for his charm and womanizing, Aronow was described as “brutally handsome” and a “ladies’ man.” Stories abound of his affairs, with an upstairs apartment above his office dubbed a “famous” rendezvous spot. At his funeral, powerboat racer Doc Magoon eulogized him as “a man’s man—and a ladies’ man,” prompting knowing glances among attendees. His relationships strained marriages and fueled his larger-than-life persona.[](https://thecaptain.tv/articles/the-wild-life-of-legendary-cigarette-builder-don-aronow/)[](http://powerandmotoryacht.com/boats/history-cigarette-founder-don-aronow/)

Aronow mingled with diverse figures: the Beatles toured Miami in a Formula boat with him, and he built boats for the Shah of Iran, Malcolm Forbes, and Charles Keating. He was also linked to mobsters and drug lords, including Ben Kramer, a racer with smuggling convictions. These connections, while lucrative, placed him in dangerous circles.[](https://thecaptain.tv/articles/the-wild-life-of-legendary-cigarette-builder-don-aronow/)[](https://www.mby.com/news/hit-mans-death-closes-notorious-aronow-case-9799)

#### Murder on Thunderboat Row: A Tragic End
On February 3, 1987, Aronow, aged 59, was shot dead in his white Mercedes on NE 188th Street, outside his USA Racing Team office. A man in a powder blue Lincoln Continental pulled alongside, fired six shots (five hit Aronow, one in the groin), and fled. Witnesses described the shooter as a tall, muscular white male with brown hair. Aronow died at Mount Sinai Medical Center at 4:45 PM.[](https://www.nytimes.com/1987/02/04/us/donald-aronow-boat-designer-and-chamion-racer-shot-to-death.html)[](https://www.marinebusinessnews.com.au/2022/03/our-history-the-risk-takers-part-3-don-aronow/)

The murder shocked the boating world. Theories ranged from jealous husbands to drug smugglers to business disputes. Miami-Dade police investigated for six years, interviewing over 100 suspects. In 1995, career criminal Robert “Bobby” Young admitted to the shooting, pleading no contest to second-degree murder and confessing fully in 2009 before his death. Young claimed he was paid $60,000 by Ben Kramer, who had a dispute with Aronow over the USA Racing Team sale. Kramer, already serving life for drug smuggling and a failed 1990 helicopter prison escape, pleaded no contest to manslaughter in 1996.[](https://www.mby.com/news/hit-mans-death-closes-notorious-aronow-case-9799)[](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Aronow)

The case remains murky. Some believe Kramer’s motive was financial—Aronow repurchased the team after the Customs Service refused to work with Kramer due to his criminal record. Others, including Aronow’s son Michael, dispute Kramer’s involvement, suggesting a personal grudge or another party. The lack of a professional hitman’s precision (e.g., the shooter lingered in Gasoline Alley) fueled speculation of a crime of passion.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1987/02/08/the-violent-death-of-an-american-dream/0419ae48-585a-460b-b62f-98cb0d50fddb/)[](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/industries-culture-society/who-shot-don-aronow-44036)

#### Legacy and Impact
Aronow’s boats transformed powerboating. His deep-V hulls, pioneered with Wynne and Walters, set the standard for speed and stability, influencing modern designs. His brands won 11 world championships, 25 U.S. championships, and 25 speed records. Cigarette, in particular, became synonymous with offshore racing and Miami’s 1980s excess, immortalized in pop culture.[](https://www.offshoreonly.com/forums/general-boating-discussion/74040-aronow-nutshell.html)

His business model—building innovative boats, racing them to prove their worth, and selling the company—created a cycle of innovation but drew criticism for lacking long-term commitment. Designer Michael Peters, who worked with Aronow, called him a mentor but also “an asshole,” reflecting his polarizing nature. Aronow’s mentorship of underdogs like Peters shaped the next generation of builders.[](http://powerandmotoryacht.com/blogs/learning-the-boat-business-from-the-legendary-don-aronow/)

Controversially, his boats’ use by smugglers linked him to Miami’s drug wars. While he cooperated with Customs officials, rumors of money laundering or mob ties persisted, though unproven. His death, dramatized in the 2009 documentary *Thunder Man: The Don Aronow Story* and the 2018 film *Speed Kills* (starring John Travolta), remains a symbol of the era’s volatility.[](https://www.mby.com/news/hit-mans-death-closes-notorious-aronow-case-9799)[](http://youreduchoices.blogspot.com/2018/06/donald-aronow.html)
