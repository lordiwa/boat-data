## Grok

Tuvalu, a remote Pacific island nation with limited infrastructure, does not have any formal yacht clubs or marinas. Yachting and cruising are possible but uncommon, with only a handful of visiting yachts each year, typically en route between places like Tonga and the Marshall Islands. Most activity centers on Funafuti Atoll (the capital), where yachts clear customs and immigration upon arrival (no fees during business hours) and anchor in the large, protected Te Namo Lagoon. Pilotage is compulsory for entry, and advance notice isn't required, but permits are needed to visit outer atolls. Anchorages are generally in 10-20 meters over sand, with a dinghy dock available near the main wharf.

However, there is an informal "yacht club" spot in Funafuti—essentially a beach hangout area where visiting sailors (yachties) gather, leave mementos like flags or signs, and relax. It's not an organized club with memberships or facilities but a casual cruiser tradition, often mentioned in sailing blogs and social media posts. The adjacent beach is noted for its soft, white sand and clear waters.

For chartering or organized yacht trips to explore the islands, options include companies like Neverland Yacht Charter, which can arrange catamarans, excursions, or sport fishing from Funafuti. If you're planning a visit, resources like Noonsite provide detailed cruising guides, and local boat harbors (primarily for small workboats) are being upgraded but aren't suited for yachts.
