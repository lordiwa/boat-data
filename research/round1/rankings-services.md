## Hub profiles

**Palma (Mallorca, Spain)**
- Two principal yards: STP Shipyard Palma and Astilleros de Mallorca (adjacent, function as a combined refit district).
- STP: 130,000 m² site, 53 berths to 120 m, 6 Travel Lifts (30T-1,000T — the only 1,000-tonne Travel Lift in Europe per STP), 7 dry-dock pits; "open yard" model lets owners choose their own contractors; >1,200 yachts/yr serviced (all sizes/scopes, not all full refits); recent €8M expansion added 5 berths to 110 m.
- Astilleros de Mallorca: 23,000 m² operational area, 4 slipways, up to 1,700-ton lifting capacity; separate dry-dock area ~55,000 m², yacht lift to 75 m.
- Spain overall captures ~20% of global refits for yachts 30 m+, ranked just behind the USA; Balearic nautical sector = 800+ companies, €1.1bn annual turnover, 5,000+ jobs, 3.1% of regional GDP; 45% of Balearic nautical companies are based in Palma itself.
- Specialties: paint (Pinmar/Rolling Stock HQ here), full-trade refit ecosystem, owner/contractor flexibility.
- Sources: [STP Shipyard Palma](https://www.stp-palma.com/en/facilities), [Astilleros de Mallorca](https://www.astillerosmallorca.com/en/about-us/facilities), [Hill Robinson](https://hillrobinson.com/news/2026/why-palma-is-still-a-leading-hub-for-superyacht-refit-and-maintenance).

**Barcelona, Spain**
- Anchor yard: MB92 Barcelona — widely cited as the largest single superyacht refit yard in the world.
- Facility: 126,500 m² total site; 220 m dry dock; 2,000-tonne Syncrolift; 4,800-tonne Shiplift (2019, described as the largest lifting facility built specifically for superyachts); 30,000 m² hardstanding (9 yachts); 14 berths to 200 m LOA.
- Throughput: >100 superyachts/yr, 35-180 m LOA.
- Also home to PTW Shipyard Barcelona (smaller refit/repair operator).
- Specialties: large-yacht dry-dock/shiplift refits, structural work, paint (Pinmar operates a facility here too).
- Sources: [MB92 Barcelona shipyard](https://mb92.com/barcelona/shipyard), [Boat International](https://www.boatinternational.com/boat-presents/celebrating-25-years-of-world-class-refits-at-mb92--32773).

**La Ciotat, France**
- Anchor yard: La Ciotat Shipyards, home to "La Grande Forme" dry dock (200 m x 60 m, extendable to 300 m; enlarged to accommodate yachts up to 200 m) and described as having the Mediterranean's biggest cranes.
- Self-reported volume: ~150 yachts/yr pass through, of which ~100 are 50 m+; claims 8-10% of the global superyacht refit market and ~1/7th of the global superyacht fleet (yard-reported figures, not independently audited — treat as a strong claim, not a verified statistic).
- MB92 Group has taken a stake/operating role at La Ciotat (MB92 La Ciotat) with plans for a large investment to build what is described as the Mediterranean's largest and most sophisticated refit hub, including exclusive use of an upcoming 4,000-tonne Shiplift.
- Specialties: large-yacht dry-dock refits, paint (Pinmar present), structural/hull work.
- Sources: [La Ciotat Shipyards](https://www.laciotat-shipyards.com/en/la-ciotat-shipyards/), [Boat International](https://www.boatinternational.com/yachts/news/la-ciotat-shipyards-reveals-refit-expansion-plans--35153), [MB92 La Ciotat](https://mb92.com/mb92-la-ciotat-awarded-exclusive-use-of-23000sqm-on-upcoming-4000-t-shiplift/).

**Fort Lauderdale / West Palm Beach, Florida, USA**
- Marketed regionally as "the yachting refit and repair capital of the world"; South Florida marine industry contributes an estimated $9B (Broward County) / $12B (South Florida region) economic impact per the Marine Industries Association of South Florida (MIASF) — this is total marine-industry economic impact, not a refit-specific figure.
- Key yards: Safe Harbor Lauderdale Marine Center (self-described largest US yacht repair shipyard, 65 acres, lifts 75-485 ton, Foreign-Trade Zone — one reported year saw 693 "yard periods" for 100ft+ vessels, 532 distinct vessels, 103 paint jobs); Bradford Marine (self-described world's largest undercover refit facility); Safe Harbor Rybovich in West Palm Beach (660-ton Travel Lift + 3,000-ton floating dry dock); Derecktor (Dania Beach — 900-ton + 220-ton Cimolai hoists; and Ft. Pierce — 1,500-ton Cimolai hoist, the largest strap-lift haul-out on record, used on the 1,300-ton yacht Coral Ocean in 2022); RMK Merrill-Stevens; Roscioli Yachting Center (now under Bradford); Jones Superyacht Miami (1,600-ton floating dry dock).
- Specialties: strap-lift/mobile-hoist dry-docking (a US-region differentiator vs. Mediterranean floating-dock/shiplift culture), MTU/CAT engine service network (RPM Diesel, Stewart & Stevenson, Ingalls Marine Diesel), teak/carpentry (Teak Techs, Wood Chuck Workshop), electronics/AV (Concord Marine Electronics, Marine Electronics Installers).
- Note: the specific yard capacity figures above (Rybovich, LMC) originate from the pre-existing local corpus (`knowledge/16_USA_Superyacht_Haul_Out_Yards_Florida_Focus.md`, a Grok-AI-assisted export) and were not independently re-verified against primary shipyard sources in this research pass — flagged in Coverage notes.
- Sources: [MIASF via GFL Alliance](https://www.gflalliance.org/information-center/marine-industries), [WorkBoat](https://www.workboat.com/shipbuilding/derecktor-sets-record-for-largest-yacht-haul-out), local corpus `knowledge/16_...md`.

**Viareggio / Livorno, Italy**
- Anchor group: Azimut|Benetti — Benetti's Livorno shipyard (~260,000 m²) described in industry press as "the world's largest active shipyard" (a new-build + refit combined claim, not dry-dock-specific), servicing refit/repair to 120 m.
- Refit division: Lusben (Azimut|Benetti group), founded in Viareggio 1956, celebrating 70 years in 2026; operates across three sites — Livorno, Viareggio, and Varazze — for vessels 30 m to 100 m+.
- Other builders/yards in the Viareggio cluster: Perini Navi/Picchiotti (sailing-yacht refit specialist), Sanlorenzo.
- Specialties: sailing-superyacht refit (Perini Navi legacy), large-scale new-build-adjacent refit capacity (Benetti/Lusben).
- Sources: [Pressmare/Lusben](https://pressmare.it/en/media/press-mare/2026-07-03/lusben-70-years-refit-from-viareggio-development-livorno-hub-90891), [Azimut|Benetti shipyards](https://www.azimutbenetti.com/our-shipyards/).

**Genoa, Italy**
- Anchor yard: Amico & Co — family business tracing to 1799, one of the oldest continuously-operating names in the sector.
- Facility: 60,000 m² total (43,000 m² land / 20,000 m² water); 10 covered work slots with centralised utilities/heating/overspray recovery; 4,000-tonne ShipLift (to 95 m LOA); 4 dry docks to 170 m LOA, including two covered docks (102 m and 90 m, 31 m air draught); Amico secured full/exclusive control of Genoa's dry dock.
- Volume: 130+ projects/yr, of which 8-10/yr are high-complexity refits (major paint, engine/generator overhauls, propulsion upgrades).
- Sustainability note: electrified docks since 2007; photovoltaic plant covers 53% of energy needs (relevant if DataYacht later tracks yard ESG data).
- Sources: [Amico & Co facilities](https://amicoshipyard.com/large-yacht-facilities/), [Yachting Pages](https://www.yachting-pages.com/articles/amico-co-confirm-use-of-new-dry-dock.html), [Boat International interview](https://www.boatinternational.com/boat-presents/superyacht-refit-repair-shipyard-genoa-interview).

## Coverage notes (weakest claims)

- **No single authoritative "best dry dock" ranking exists.** The rankings table mixes three incompatible types of
  superlative (raw commercial engineering scale, yacht-specific engineering scale, and industry "best refit project"
  awards). Treat row groupings, not a single ordinal rank, as the deliverable.
- **"Busiest refit hub" by yacht count is largely self-reported.** La Ciotat's ~150 yachts/yr and "8-10% of global
  market" figures come from the yard's own marketing material, not an independent industry audit. No independently
  audited per-hub annual refit-count comparison (Palma vs. Barcelona vs. La Ciotat vs. Fort Lauderdale vs. Viareggio
  vs. Genoa) was found. The SYBAss/Deloitte/VU Amsterdam study gives Mediterranean-wide economic figures (€4.7bn
  opex + €1.9bn tourism spend) but not a per-hub refit-count breakdown; the full Boat International "Refit report"
  (Oct 2024) is paywalled and could not be read beyond its landing page.
- **RM Yacht Painting** (named explicitly in the research brief) could not be corroborated by any primary source in
  this pass — no working company website or independent profile was found. It is included in the service-companies
  table as an open item; do not treat it as confirmed.
- **G&K Yachting** appears only via the Boat International WSA 2025 winners page as "refit yard/management" for
  Ravenger; no independent company profile (HQ city, website) was found this pass.
- **Florida yard capacity figures** (Safe Harbor Rybovich's 660-ton lift / 3,000-ton dry dock; Lauderdale Marine
  Center's 485-ton lift and 693-yard-period statistic) rely on the pre-existing local corpus file
  `knowledge/16_USA_Superyacht_Haul_Out_Yards_Florida_Focus.md`, which is itself a Grok-AI-assisted export with its
  own caveats about needing direct yard confirmation. These were not re-verified against primary shipyard sources
  in this pass and should be independently confirmed before being treated as canonical DataYacht facts.
- **"World's largest shiplift" claim for Astilleros Canarios (Las Palmas)** comes from a single TRB/TRID
  transportation-research abstract; it may be dated (undetermined publication year) and was not cross-checked
  against a current, dated source — treat as historical/possibly superseded.
- **No entries found** for a distinct "Boat International Refit Yard of the Year" award as a named, standalone,
  recurring category — Boat International's refit recognition is folded into the World Superyacht Awards' "Refit"
  yacht category, which credits the refit yacht and its refit yard/management jointly, not a yard alone.
