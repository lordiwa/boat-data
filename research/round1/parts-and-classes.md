## Size classes

There is **no single official legal definition** for "superyacht", "megayacht", or "gigayacht" — they are industry/marketing labels, not statutory classes. The only hard regulatory line is 24 metres load line length (see Regulatory thresholds). Below, competing conventions are shown side by side rather than collapsed into one answer.

| Class | Length Threshold | GT Range | Typical Crew | Definition Used By | Example Vessels | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boat / Yacht | <24m (<79ft) | <500 GT | 0-8 (often owner-operated below ~15m) | General industry usage (YachtBuyer, YachtWorld) | Typical 12-23m flybridge motor yacht or cruising sailboat | Below the 24m Load Line/REG Yacht Code trigger; may be run without professional crew. |
| Superyacht | 24m+ (79ft+); some sources start at 30m (98ft) | ~500-3,000 GT (varies) | 3-16 depending on size | Most common convention (YachtBuyer, Burgess, Camper & Nicholsons legacy usage); SYBAss uses 40m+ (motor) / 30m+ (sailing) for its own membership bar, not as an industry-wide definition | 24-30m: entry-level superyachts; up to ~60m e.g. Amels 60, Heesen 50m models | Conflict: some outlets (Boat International, Grok-sourced corpus) put the practical "true superyacht" start at 30m rather than 24m, treating 24-30m as a grey zone. |
| Megayacht | ~60m+ (197ft+) by one convention; ~80m+ (260ft+) by another | ~3,000-8,000 GT (YachtBuyer); "3,000 GT" cited generally as the dividing line (Burgess) | 15-50+; Burgess cites "minimum of 25 crew" for 80m+ | YachtBuyer (60m+); Burgess (80m+ or 3,000 GT); term coined in the 1980s by George Nicholson of Camper & Nicholsons | Rising Sun (138m/7,841 GT), Octopus (126m/9,932 GT), Dubai (162m/12,488 GT) | Direct conflict: 60m vs 80m as the superyacht/megayacht boundary — no consensus; "megayacht" and "superyacht" are also frequently used interchangeably in casual/media usage regardless of size. |
| Gigayacht | 90m+ (295ft+) by one convention; 100m+ (328ft+) by another | 8,000+ GT (YachtBuyer); Grok-sourced corpus cites >10,000 GT | 35-100+ (est. ~150 vessels worldwide fit this tier) | YachtBuyer/Rightboat/Megayacht News generally use 100m; some brokers (e.g. cited via Camper & Nicholsons-adjacent sources) use 90m | Azzam (180.6m/13,136 GT), Dilbar (156m/15,917 GT), Eclipse (162.5m/13,564 GT), REV Ocean (194.9m, delivery expected 2026/2027) | Conflict: 90m vs 100m threshold. Also note "longest" and "largest by GT" are different rankings — Azzam is longest, Dilbar has more GT despite being 24.6m shorter. |

## Coverage notes

- **Knowledge base**: no `knowledge/entries/` or `knowledge/graph/graph.json` exist yet in this repo (only the raw `/knowledge` Grok-export corpus used to seed DataYacht v1), so the mandatory KB lookup produced zero candidates — `kb_hits` is empty and this is pure web research. The existing corpus file `knowledge/05_Grok_Shipyard_Research_for_DataYacht_Reports.md`-style file `knowledge/28_Superyachts_Definition_Features_Innovations.md` was read as background but is an unsourced AI chat transcript, not treated as a citable source here; every claim above traces to an independent web source instead.
- **Weakest data**: (1) GT figures for named gigayachts vary slightly between outlets by a few hundred GT (rounding/registry differences) — treated as directionally correct, not exact to the ton. (2) The 60m-vs-80m megayacht boundary and 90m-vs-100m gigayacht boundary are genuinely unresolved in the industry, not a research gap on our end — shown as explicit conflicts rather than picked. (3) Crew-count-by-length figures are informal industry rules of thumb, not a cited regulation, and vary by vessel complexity (explorer/expedition yachts need more crew than a flybridge cruiser of the same length). (4) Rig & sail and interior categories lean on general marine glossaries rather than superyacht-specific ones for a few entries (e.g. traveller, boom vang) — accurate for sailing yachts generally but not verified against a superyacht-specific source.
- **Not covered / open for round 2**: detailed helipad certification classes (CAP 437 vs FAA), classification-society (Lloyd's, ABS, DNV, RINA, Bureau Veritas) notation differences, MLC 2006 crew-welfare thresholds, and a part-by-part sourced glossary specific to explorer/expedition yachts (ice-class hulls, moon pools) — flagged for a follow-up research lane if DataYacht wants explorer-yacht coverage.
