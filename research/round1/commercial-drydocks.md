## Coverage notes

- **Weakest data**: Philippines (Agila Subic — dimension sources conflict sharply, e.g. one summary claimed a 550m x 135m dock, which would be wider than any confirmed dock on earth; omitted rather than reported as fact), Malta (Palumbo commercial-side dock dimensions not confirmed beyond dock count and a note that Dock 6 handles ultra-large containerships), Enseada Brazil (no confirmed dock dimensions), Bollinger Harvey and Imabari (multi-site operators where a single "largest dock" figure is not clearly attributable to one physical dock), IMI Ras Al-Khair (conflicting synchrolift/ship-lift tonnage figures across sources, left blank).
- **Founded dates** are frequently unknown or ambiguous where a yard has been renamed/reflagged multiple times (state shipyards in China, Gulf JVs, several Turkish Tuzla-cluster yards) — left blank rather than guessed.
- **Tonnage figures** mix DWT, GT, and lift-capacity (long tons) conventions across sources depending on whether the dock is a graving dock (rated by vessel DWT/GT) or floating dock (rated by lift capacity); where the source didn't specify a maximum for the largest dock at a site, the cell was left blank instead of conflating a different dock's capacity.
- **Websites**: several state-owned or JV yards (Skaramangas, Neorion Syros, Sedef, TK Tuzla, Besiktas partially, Suez, MEC Balboa, San Giorgio del Porto, Chantier Naval de Marseille) do not have a clearly citable primary corporate URL distinct from directory aggregator sites (TrustedDocks, shipyards.gr) — left blank to avoid citing a non-authoritative source as the "Website."
