# TASK-016 Research Lane B — Independent Superyacht Refit, Repair & Haul-Out Yards Worldwide

Scope: physical facilities that perform superyacht/megayacht refit, repair, haul-out or dry-docking and are **not** owned by a yacht builder. A few yards explicitly named in the research brief (Lusben, Icon Yachts, Huisfit/Royal Huisman) are in fact builder-affiliated or builder-owned; they are included per the brief's explicit instruction but flagged in Coverage notes as a scope exception. Commercial/naval shipyards that also service megayachts are marked `commercial + yacht refit`.

All figures are the max verified figures found in primary/official sources or reputable trade press (Boat International, Superyacht Times, Superyacht Services Guide, TrustedDocks, official shipyard sites). Cells are left blank rather than guessed where no figure was found.

## Coverage notes

- **Builder-ownership scope exception.** The research brief explicitly named three facilities that are, on investigation, builder-owned or builder-affiliated and therefore technically outside the Lane B "not owned by a builder" definition: **Lusben** (part of the Azimut\|Benetti Group), **Huisfit** (Royal Huisman's own refit division), and **Icon Yachts** (itself an explorer-yacht builder that also does third-party refit/conversion work). They are included per the brief's explicit instruction and flagged here so the Orchestrator can decide whether to keep, relabel, or move them to the builder-owned lane.
- **"Oldenburger"** as named in the brief could not be matched to any physical shipyard; the only matching entity found is Oldenburger Interior GmbH & Co. KG, a luxury interior fit-out company (yachts, aircraft, residences), not a haul-out/refit facility. Excluded from the table. If a different "Oldenburger" shipyard was intended, it needs a corrected name from the requester.
- **Turkey Tuzla cluster.** "Sea Alliance" and "Zeytinoğlu" (named in the brief) could not be confirmed as distinct, currently active Tuzla shipyards in any primary or trade-press source searched; TK Tuzla Shipyard and KRM Yacht are the two best-documented Tuzla-area facilities and are included instead. These two names should be re-verified with a Turkish-language source or the requester before being dropped entirely.
- **Weak/low-confidence data points**, flagged individually in their Notes cells above: Palumbo Rijeka (capacity data thin), Iskra Shipyard Šibenik (specs not independently corroborated), ASRY Bahrain and Asyad/Oman Drydock Duqm (primarily commercial/naval yards; superyacht-specific activity unconfirmed), Seatrium Admiralty Yard Singapore (commercial complex, yacht-refit volume undocumented), Ratanachai Shipyard Phuket (official site and directory figures disagree on max LOA/tonnage), and the Cape Town V&A Waterfront superyacht marina (new marina still under construction as of this research; existing refit infrastructure is shared commercial-port equipment, not a purpose-built yacht yard yet).
- **Regions with thinner coverage than the rest of the report:** Sub-Saharan Africa (only Cape Town found; no confirmed independent yard elsewhere on the continent), Central/South America outside the Caribbean (no qualifying independent superyacht-scale yard surfaced), and mainland China / South Korea / Japan (not in scope of the brief's named list and not separately researched here).
- **Dock Dimensions and Dry Docks counts** are left blank wherever a facility's public materials describe capacity only in tonnage/LOA terms rather than physical dock length/width, per the "never guess" instruction — this affects several Monaco Marine sites, Palumbo Rijeka, Seatrium, and a few others.
- **Consolidation activity to watch:** Bradford Marine acquired Roscioli Yachting Center (Oct 2023) and now operates it as part of the same Fort Lauderdale facility; Rybovich and Lauderdale Marine Center, Savannah Yacht Center, Front Street Shipyard and Newport Shipyard are all now under the Safe Harbor Marinas ownership umbrella, which is itself a marina-operator roll-up rather than a yacht builder, so these remain in-scope for Lane B.
